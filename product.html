The problem

You've installed a blocker before. You turned it off in four seconds.

That's not a discipline failure — it's a design failure. A blocker with an off switch is a reminder, and reminders lose to a craving every time. The moment you most want the app open is the exact moment the app lets you open it.

Unracked closes that gap. Blocking is enforced by iOS itself, so quitting Unracked does nothing. Schedules and places arm without you. And the way out is intentionally slow enough that you have to actually decide to take it.

Three ways to block

Start it yourself, or never think about it again.

Most people use all three. The point is that the ones you don't start are the ones that work when your judgement is worst.

Focus sessions

One tap, and the noise is gone

Block for 15 minutes, two hours, or until you decide you're done. A live ring counts it down in the app, in the Dynamic Island, and on your Lock Screen — and finishing one builds your streak.

Schedules

The same apps gone, every week

Weekday mornings. Every evening. All night. Set the window once and the block arms itself on time, every time, even with Unracked closed. Nothing to remember, nothing to start.

Locations

Blocking starts when you walk in

Pick a place and the block begins the moment you arrive, then lifts when you leave. Your desk, a classroom, a gym, a library, home. It works with your phone in your pocket and the app shut.

Built to hold

Every shortcut around it, closed on purpose.

  • Force-quitting Unracked does nothing. Blocking runs on Apple's Screen Time framework inside iOS, not on Unracked staying alive in the background.
  • Restarting your phone does nothing. The shield is back up before you reach the Home Screen.
  • Schedules and locations run with the app closed. iOS wakes Unracked at the boundary — the block doesn't wait for you to open anything.
  • Every early exit costs the same ten seconds. Deleting a schedule mid-block isn't a cheaper way out than ending a session. There is no cheaper way out.
  • You can always see what's running. The live block shows in the Dynamic Island and on your Lock Screen, so a block is never a mystery.
  • Streaks count only what you finished. Sessions you bailed on don't count, which is the entire point of counting them.

The ten-second hold

Not locked out. Just slowed down.

A blocker you genuinely cannot escape is a liability — phones matter, emergencies happen. So Unracked always gives you the door. It just makes you hold it open.

Ten seconds with your thumb on the screen is long enough that the impulse that started the reach has usually passed before the block lifts. And because the cost is identical for a session, a schedule, or a place, there's no clever workaround to discover — only the decision.

Hold to end

Release early and the block stays exactly where it was.

Private by design

There's no account, because there's nothing to store.

No sign-up. No email. Your blocked apps, your schedules and your places never leave your phone.

Apple's Screen Time hands Unracked anonymous tokens instead of app names, so even the developer can't tell which apps you picked. Locations are matched on the device — never logged, never transmitted.

Questions

The things people ask first.

What happens if I force-quit Unracked while a block is running?

Nothing. The shield stays up. Blocking is enforced by iOS through Apple's Screen Time framework, not by Unracked running in the background — so closing the app has no effect, and neither does restarting your phone.

Can I stop a block early?

Yes, with a deliberate ten-second press-and-hold. It costs the same whether the block came from a focus session, a schedule, or arriving at a place. Unracked is a commitment device, not a trap: there's always a way out, it's just never a reflex.

Do schedules and locations work when the app is closed?

Yes. Schedules arm and disarm themselves with Unracked closed, and location blocks start the moment you arrive and lift when you leave, whether or not the app is open.

Why does Unracked need Screen Time access?

Screen Time — Family Controls — is the only mechanism Apple provides for blocking apps on iPhone, and Unracked uses it on your own device. In exchange, Apple hands the app anonymous tokens instead of app names, so it never learns what you blocked.

Why does location blocking need Always location access?

So a block can start when you arrive without the app being open. iOS wakes Unracked at the boundary of a place you chose. The match happens on the device; your location is never logged and never transmitted.

What happens if my subscription ends?

Blocking stops. Schedules and locations disarm and any running block is released, so a lapsed subscription can never lock you out of your own apps. Your settings are kept, and resubscribing restores them.

Which devices are supported?

iPhone, running iOS 26 or later. There's no iPad, Mac, Android or web version.
